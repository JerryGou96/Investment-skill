# JJ-Macro NFP Framework Reference

## Two-survey split

### Establishment Survey
Use for employer-side labor demand:
- Headline nonfarm payrolls
- Private payrolls vs government payrolls
- Industry breadth and cyclicality
- Average hourly earnings MoM/YoY
- Average weekly hours
- Prior-month revisions

### Household Survey
Use for labor-market slack and unemployment-rate mechanics:
- Civilian employment level
- Unemployment level
- Civilian labor force
- Not in labor force
- Labor force participation rate
- Employment-population ratio
- U-6
- Part-time for economic reasons

## Unemployment-rate decomposition logic

Unemployment rate = unemployed persons / labor force.

Interpretation matrix:

| Pattern | Interpretation | Macro read |
|---|---|---|
| Unemployment rate up + employed up + labor force up strongly | Labor supply returning | Usually not recessionary; can be disinflationary |
| Unemployment rate up + employed down + unemployed up | Demand weakening | Dovish Fed impulse, but growth-risk negative |
| Unemployment rate down/stable + not-in-labor-force up | Hidden weakness | Headline rate overstates strength |
| Payrolls strong + household employment weak | Survey divergence | Market may trade headline first, then reassess quality |
| Payrolls broad strong + wages hot + hours firm | Reacceleration | Hawkish rates/dollar risk |
| Payrolls weak + U-6/part-time economic reasons up | Quality deterioration | Hard-landing risk rises |

## Market translation guide

- **Goldilocks**: payrolls okay, unemployment stable, wages cool -> equities/risk assets supportive, yields contained.
- **Hawkish strong**: payrolls very strong, wages hot, unemployment down for healthy reasons -> yields/dollar up, duration pressure.
- **Dovish soft landing**: payrolls slow, wages cool, unemployment up due to labor supply -> rate-cut probability up without recession panic.
- **Growth scare**: payrolls weak, household employment down, unemployment/U-6 up -> yields may fall, equities mixed/negative, defensives outperform.
- **Hidden weakness**: unemployment stable due to labor-force exit -> watch consumption, confidence, and future payroll revisions.
